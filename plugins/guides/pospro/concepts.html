{N}How a trajectory is made

A trajectory is the position and orientation of the Certus at every moment of the drive. PosPro builds it from two kinds of measurement that fail in opposite ways, and from what you tell it about the vehicle. Knowing how they fit together explains every setting in this guide and why the steps come in the order they do.

Two sensors that cover for each other

GNSS (the two antennas)IMU (inside the Certus)
MeasuresDistances to satellites: code (metres) and carrier phase (millimetres, once its whole number of cycles, the ambiguity, is known)Acceleration and turn rate, 200 times a second
StrengthErrors do not grow with time: centimetres anywhere the sky is openSmooth, never drops out, gives roll, pitch and heading
WeaknessBlocked by bridges, trees, buildings; carrier phase must be fixed again after every interruptionErrors grow with time when nothing corrects them (metres after a minute)

The Kalman filter combines them: the IMU carries the trajectory from one moment to the next, and every GNSS measurement pulls it back to where the satellites say it is. In tight coupling (PosPro's default) the filter uses each satellite's raw observations from both antennas directly, so even two or three satellites under trees still help; loose coupling only uses complete GNSS positions.

Differential: the base station

Most GNSS errors (satellite orbits and clocks, the ionosphere and troposphere) are almost the same for two receivers a few kilometres apart. Processing the rover's observations against a base station's (double differences, PPK) removes them and makes the carrier-phase ambiguities fixable: that is what turns metres into centimetres. Two consequences:

  • The whole trajectory sits on the base station's position: an error there moves every point by the same amount. That is why the base position is computed carefully (PPP or CSRS-PPP), not taken from the RINEX header.
  • Accuracy falls with distance to the base, about 1 cm + 1 mm per km (1 ppm), and fixing gets harder beyond 20–30 km.

Forward, backward and combined

A filter only knows the past. Driving forward, it enters a GNSS gap well known and leaves it with its largest error; running the same data backward, the opposite. Combining the two passes (weighting each by its own accuracy) gives the smallest error everywhere, and halfway through a gap it is much better than either. The separation between the passes is also the most honest quality check there is: two independent solutions that agree to 2 cm are right to about 2 cm; where they disagree, one of them has a wrong fix.

{N}The workflow and why it is in this order

PosPro's eight steps are grouped in three phases. Each step depends on the ones before it, and the step bar on the left, the Next button and the step notes guide you through them in order.

StepWhy it comes here
1 DataEverything else is computed from the rover files. Reading them first shows how complete they are (IMU and satellite coverage, gaps in the recording) before any time is spent on them.
2 InstallationThe antenna offsets are used in every epoch of processing, and a wrong offset (or swapped antennas) cannot be fixed afterwards without processing again. They come straight from the capture, so this is mostly a check, and Check antennas confirms them from the data itself.
3 BaseThe base position decides where the whole trajectory sits. It is computed (PPP) before processing, because every PPK solution is relative to it.
4 ProcessingRuns once everything it needs is right.
5 QualityJudges the result before anything is built on it: the share of fixed ambiguities, the forward/backward separation and the gaps say whether to go on, change a setting, or check the inputs.
6 CalibrationSolving the lever arms needs a processed drive and means processing again. Done after a first good processing and before any review, it leaves no hand edits to lose.
7 ReviewHand edits belong to one processed trajectory, so they come last; processing again takes hand selections off the list.
8 ExportOnly for other software: AlignPro and CloudPro already read the trajectory PosPro publishes every time it processes or applies a review edit.
Most jobs With good data the path is short: Data (read), Installation (check), Base (add, PPP), Processing (process), Quality (look), and on to AlignPro. Calibration is for a new installation; Review only where Quality points to a problem.

{N}Best practices

Most of a trajectory's accuracy is decided before PosPro runs: by how the data were recorded. These are the practices used across the industry for GNSS/INS mobile mapping, and how PosPro supports each.

In the field

PracticeWhy
Measure the antenna and Lidar offsets to the Certus reference point to 1 cm, and enter them in the mapper (Settings › Hardware)Offsets go straight into every position. PosPro reads them from the capture; Check antennas confirms GPS1 and GPS2 from the data.

Drive a few turns and figure-eights early onMakes heading and the IMU biases observable; the lever-arm solver needs turns.
Log the base for the whole drive plus 10 minutes before and after, at 1 s; within 10–20 km where possibleThe base must cover every rover epoch; accuracy falls about 1 mm per km; 1 s matches the rover's whole-second epochs.
Record the mapper's navigation recording (mapper 2.10), not only the survey linesOne continuous recording from start to end processes as one trajectory that covers every line, static periods included.
Use the mapper's recommended Certus outputsRaw IMU at 200 Hz and raw observations of both antennas at 8 Hz on whole seconds are what tight coupling needs (see Recording settings).
Avoid long GNSS outages where you can; pass through them at a steady speedIMU-only error grows quickly with time; the wheel constraints help most at constant speed.
