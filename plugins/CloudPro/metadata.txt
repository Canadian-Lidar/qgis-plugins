description=Point cloud georeferencing, cleaning, classification and delivery for the Canadian Lidar mobile mapper: stage 03 of the Data Collection Systems chain (PosPro, AlignPro, CloudPro).
about=CloudPro opens the job's shared processing project, georeferences every Lidar return of the mapper's .clraw captures at its own firing time with the trajectory PosPro published and the mounting AlignPro published (or the mounting recorded in the capture, marked "no calibration"), and gives fast manual and automatic cleaning: a four-quarter layout (Plan with the project grid and a rotatable Inspection Area, Slice for cross-sections and profiles, interactive 3D, automatic filters), an icon tool bar that can float, editing by rectangle, polygon, lasso, brush and above/below a drawn line (reject, accept, select, classify, inside or outside), a Project Properties window (information, geodetics, survey configuration, project files), exact stepping navigation, and 36 automatic filters in groups with live preview (cell statistics, clip, isolated cluster, cloth ground, statistical outliers, snow and rain removal, morphological ground filters, eigenvalue features, wall and reflection detection, height above ground classes, multi-pass moving-object removal, and feature extraction of road markings, curbs, poles, traffic signs, power lines, manhole covers and guardrails). When PosPro or AlignPro publish again, Update georeferencing rebuilds the points and carries every edit over. Publishes LAS 1.4, a ground grid and cloud.json for the job. Needs the DCS Core plugin. Menu: Plugins > Canadian Lidar > Data Systems.

    2.2.2 Steady offsets corrected: along the road and sideways now come from walls only (on gently sloping ground a height difference between the passes looked like an along-road shift - Shirley's 8.4 cm per pass was mostly that; the walls say about 3), the along-road offset is the median of the walls facing along the road, and it is also measured in four parts of the line: when it changes along the line it is reported as trajectory (PosPro), not calibration.
    2.2.1 Steady offsets: each line pair's steady offset is solved in the vehicle's own frame (along the road, sideways, up, and a roll slope that grows with the distance from the drive lines), so an offset that reverses with the driving direction is reported as calibration (AlignPro) and one between lines driven the same way as trajectory (PosPro): STEADY OFFSETS tab, report, summary sentence. Problem areas are now what is left after each pair's steady offset (Shirley's 102 areas were one steady offset cut into pieces), pieces of the same stretch a few seconds apart are one area, at least 10 m2. Problem areas: local patches where one line is off (beyond twice the tolerance), with the line at fault and the time its points were taken (UTC and GPS seconds of week), outlined and numbered on the Plan; clicking one puts the Inspection Area there; written to out/problem_areas.json for PosPro's Trajectory Review.     1.9.9 Project-wide filter runs on every core: dense tiles are split into parts of at most 2 million points (buffer shrinks to no less than 3 m where a part with its buffer would exceed 6 million), parts run in parallel threads within half the free memory; same edits as one thread. Progress shows parts and points done, parts running, points changed and time left. Cancel stops within seconds and writes nothing (it used to write the tiles already computed and record the run as done). Performance log: parts, threads, memory and loaded points per run. Themed number and rename boxes (Memory limit, Rename group) and message boxes in the Georeference dialog.
    1.9.8 Performance log and report: every Inspection Area load (with the read/order/prepare/draw breakdown and area size), filter run (with each filter's time), preview, job (georeferencing manual or automatic, tiled filters, publish, export; with result and time), how the 3D view draws (graphics card or processor, and why) and its frame times go to 03_CloudPro/log/performance.log, with this computer (processor, cores, memory, QGIS, engine) at the top of each session. MENU > Performance report... saves one text file (computer, job, the log, recent History and run logs) to send for review.
    1.9.7 3D view on the graphics card: with OpenGL 2.1 or later the loaded points are uploaded to the card once and every frame draws all of them in one call, moving or still (no thinning while turning); the engine (version 3) makes the OpenGL calls and computes the eye-dome shading on every core (also used for processor drawing). Without a usable card, with software OpenGL (llvmpipe, GDI Generic) or if the card cannot hold the points, the view draws on the processor as before; the status bar says which. CLOUDPRO_NO_GPU=1 turns the card off.
    1.9.6 Background georeferencing: when PosPro or AlignPro publish and everything upstream is current again (AlignPro is waited for after a PosPro publish unless the job uses the recorded or entered mounting), CloudPro georeferences the job with the settings of its last georeferencing and carries every edit over: in the background with a progress bar in the QGIS message bar when CloudPro's window is closed (the window opens the job when it has finished), in the window when it shows the job. Nothing is published automatically. On by default; Georeference dialog: 'Update automatically when PosPro or AlignPro publish'. Needs DCS Core 1.10.0 (API 11).
    1.9.5 Faster views on big areas: the Plan, Slice and 3D views are drawn by the CloudPro engine (engine 2) on every core but one, one pass over the points instead of sorting them all (25 million points on one core: Plan 2.5 to 0.4 s, 3D 1.6 to 0.25 s; identical picture at point size 1, at larger sizes the highest or nearest point always wins). Loading an Inspection Area skips tiles outside the rotated area, takes tiles inside it whole, tests the rest in the engine and reads tiles in parallel; the random point order is built in parallel. The status bar gives the loading time by step (read, order, prepare, draw) and loads over 2 s are noted in the History.
    1.9.4 Export thinning and tiles: 'Thin to' a grid spacing from a list (0.02 to 5 m, the point density shown beside each; 3D cells keep walls and poles, 2D plan cells for ground models; the real point nearest each cell centre is kept, coordinates and attributes untouched) and 'Tiles' of 50 to 1000 m named by their lower-left corner, for LAS and XYZ, also with one file per line, and on the command line (--spacing, --thin-2d, --tile); the grid surface cell is a list too. Much faster filters on dense data: Snow / rain / dust (DROR, LIOR, DIOR) and Radius outlier decide from the nearest neighbours instead of counting every neighbour within the radius (thousands per point on dense facades), with an exact shortcut for points in dense cells: their cost no longer grows with the density, results identical. SOR is computed in blocks (bounded memory). Run stops a live preview still computing (two runs at once shared the cores and doubled the memory) and reuses a finished preview of the same group and points instead of computing it again. Area runs report the time of each filter in the filter panel and the History log.
    1.9.3 Upgrades work while QGIS is open: the CloudPro engine library is loaded from a copy in the user's folder (%LOCALAPPDATA%\CloudPro\engine on Windows, ~/.cache/CloudPro/engine on Linux), so Windows no longer locks the plugin folder. In 1.9.0 to 1.9.2 an upgrade after CloudPro had loaded its engine failed with 'Failed to remove the directory' and left CloudPro without classFactory.
    1.9.2 Clearer editing: the selection shape (rectangle, polygon, lasso, brush, above / below) is red for Reject, green for Accept, grey for Select; rejected points are shown in magenta (and no longer listed on the scale bars); the Inspection Area outline is white. Window tabs along the bottom; view titles without point counts (the status bar keeps the counts); the Plan cell size is a list of the available sizes. 3D: left drag and the left / right arrows turn the cloud the way of the movement (the near side moves with the mouse).
    1.9.1 Navigation: every view has a Zoom to the Inspection Area button and the Plan a Zoom to the extents button; the 3D view turns around the centre of the Inspection Area (at the median height of its points) and the centre follows the area when it is moved or stepped (pan or double-click still move it; the button, 0 or Home bring it back); the 3D navigation arrows turn 22.5 degrees per click (holding still turns smoothly). Tall outliers no longer push the 3D zoom far out. Colour range per view: right-click a scale bar for automatic over the Inspection Area (default), automatic over the whole project, or a fixed minimum and maximum (heights as elevations, times in UTC); saved with the project; a linked view uses the range of the view it follows.
    1.9.0 CloudPro engine: the per-point work now runs in a small compiled library (C, Windows and Linux, in the plugin) on every core but one. Georeferencing (reading the packets, Hesai decoding, skipping stopped packets, georeferencing and sorting into tiles) is about 3 times faster per core and now uses all cores; tile grids are built in parallel; Moving objects (free-space rays), Guardrails, Poles and Signs (clusters) and Vertical plane (RANSAC) run their loops in the engine. Exactly the same points, records and filter results as before (checked record by record); the log says which code did the work. Without the library, or with CLOUDPRO_NO_ENGINE=1, CloudPro uses its Python code as before.
    1.8.1 Faster filters and grids. The morphological ground filter (SMRF) does its disk openings by rows (exactly the same ground points, about 8 times faster: 1 M points 6.2 -> 0.75 s); Moving objects counts each voxel once per ray without a full sort; the per-tile grids are binned on whole millimetres (about twice as fast, 10 M points per tile 0.94 -> 0.47 s) and after an edit are rebuilt in a background thread, so the window no longer pauses; jobs, project changes and closing wait for that rebuild to finish.
    1.8.0 Dockable windows: Plan, Slice, 3D, Automatic filters, Lines/Classes/Info and History are each a window inside CloudPro that can be moved, resized, tabbed together, floated (e.g. onto a second screen) or closed; MENU > Windows reopens them and resets the layout; the layout is remembered. A closed or hidden view is not drawn or refreshed. Rejected points are hidden by default (never deleted): each view has its own eye to show them in red, the tool bar eye (H) sets every view, and views colour and draw only the points they show. Big jobs: "Skip points while stopped" when georeferencing (on for new projects; about a third fewer points on a job parked a third of the time), tile sorting in the import threads and up to 16 threads, loaded points held in single precision (about 40% less memory per point), edits refresh the views about twice as fast (the area frame and ground level are kept per area), one Update georeferencing prompt (the header button is gone; the workflow strip has it), and the point store is checked against its index on every open and repaired after a crash or full disk. Faster 3D view: single-precision projection and nearest-point-per-pixel drawing (a still frame of 3 M points 0.33 -> 0.18 s), and the full-detail picture after a move is drawn in a background thread so the window keeps responding.
    1.7.1 Coordinate epoch (Project properties > Geodetics): the points are delivered at the provincial NAD83(CSRS) epoch - New Brunswick 1997.0 for New Brunswick Stereographic, else the federal 2010.0 - or as observed. A PPK trajectory in another epoch (a base at the observation date) or a PPP trajectory is moved with NRCan's velocity grid (about 8 cm horizontally and 2 cm vertically between 2026 and 1997.0 near Fredericton); cloud.json gives the epoch of the points. Needs DCS Core 1.9.0.
